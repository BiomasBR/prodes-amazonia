Stage 2

Image Segmentation

Partition the cube into spatially homogeneous objects

Object-Based Image Analysis (OBIA) is an approach to remote sensing image analysis that partitions an image into closed segments, which are subsequently classified and analyzed. The process begins by grouping pixels according to their similarity, based on a distance metric that considers both spatial and spectral components. Simões et al. [1] constructed a multitemporal attribute space in which each combination of time and band is treated as an independent dimension. A data cube with 10 bands and 24 time steps results in a 240-dimensional spectral space, complemented by a 2-dimensional Euclidean space representing spatial coordinates.

The spatial component measures how far a pixel is from the center of a superpixel in terms of its x and y coordinates, while the spectral component evaluates how different the pixel’s spectral values are from the average spectral signature of the superpixel across all temporal instances. As a result, the segmentation process produces superpixels corresponding to coherent object parts in the Sentinel-2 image.

Building on this concept, the first step of the OBIA procedure in sits is to select a data cube for segmentation, along with the function that will perform this task. The package provides a generic sits_segment function, enabling users to choose among different segmentation algorithms. Currently, two options are available: sits_snic, which implements the Simple Non-Iterative Clustering (SNIC) algorithm, and sits_slic, which implements an extended version of the Simple Linear Iterative Clustering (SLIC) algorithm.

In this work, we adopt the SNIC algorithm due to its efficiency and its improvements over SLIC. A comprehensive description of the method can be found in Achanta & Süsstrunk [2], where the algorithm is formally introduced and discussed. Three parameters control the output: spacing (target segment radius in pixels), compactness (trade-off between spectral and spatial homogeneity), and padding (border pixel handling).

The result of the segmentation is shown below (Figure 2.1) and demonstrates adherence of the segmentation to the one-month cube. Merging the cube was necessary because using only the fractions could lead to the omission of data visible in the optical imagery, such as deforestation with vegetation. In areas affected by clouds or artifacts gaps may appear in the segmentation. To address this, the script implements a condition that checks whether the segmentation can be completed within the July period. If not, the temporal window is progressively expanded backward (from June to July, May to July, and so on).
